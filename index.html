Outplaying disparities at the FIFA World Cup.

How have countries performed in relation to their socio-economic standing in the FIFA World Cup over time?

Every qualified team is ranked on two 0-1 scales for each tournament: a performance score (stage reached, with points, goal difference and goals scored as tiebreakers, normalised to [0, 1]) and an socio-economic score (the normalised [0, 1] mean of the ticked socio-economic features for each country on the year of each tournament). Then overperformance is calculated as performance minus economic score . A positive score means a national team performed well above its weight, while a negative score means it fell short (adjusted according to that country's underlying socio-economic conditions).

Click here to find out more about the socio-economic indicators used
Human development (HDI)
- A summary of human development. Measured 0-1: where 0 is least developed, and 1 is most developed. Composed of three dimensions: health (life expectancy), knowledge (mean and expected years of schooling), and living standard (gross national income per capita)
GDP per capita
- Total income earned through the production of goods and services. Measured in current USD.
Population
- Country population, in millions. Included as a scaling factor - not used as a direct wealth measure.
Gender parity (GDI)
- Female HDI divided by male HDI. Closer to 1 means men and women attain similar levels across health, education and income.
Gender inequality (GII)
- A summary of gender inequality: composed of measures relating to reproductive health, empowerment and labour market. Measured 0-1: a low score means low gender inequality; high means more, so the value is inverted before averaging to align with the other indicators.
Urban share
- Percentage of population living in urban areas.
School enrollment
- Percentage of total school enrollment in given age group.
